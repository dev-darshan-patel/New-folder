Bookify

A multi-tenant, Calendly-style appointment booking platform. A business owner signs up, sets their hours and “event types”, and shares one link where customers self-book a time slot — no back-and-forth emails.

How to use this guide

This single page covers three different jobs. Jump to the part that matches yours:

You want to take bookings. Start with Your first 15 minutes, then Event types. No technical knowledge needed.

I write the code

Start at Architecture, then Local setup and Conventions.

Throughout, a coloured tag shows which plan a feature needs: FREE PRO BUSINESS. These are the shipped defaults — a platform admin can change exactly what each plan includes at any time, so your own install may differ. See Plans & features.

Everything here was written from the code, not from memory. Where a number appears — a timeout, a retry limit, a grace period — it's the value the app actually uses today.

What is Bookify

Bookify lets a small business — a consultant, salon, coach, clinic, agency, tutor, anyone who takes appointments — run their own self-service scheduling page without touching code.

Each signup becomes an independent tenant: their own hours, their own bookable event types, their own branded booking page at yourdomain.com/{their-handle}, and their own customer bookings. Tenants pick a plan that unlocks more capability as they grow — from custom branding, up through team round-robin scheduling, group classes, and taking payments.

Above all the tenants sits a platform admin console at /admin for whoever runs Bookify itself: user management, revenue oversight, plan configuration, email templates, feature kill-switches, and a full audit trail.

The three surfaces

Core concepts

Five ideas explain most of the product. Everything else builds on these.

Tenant
One business account. Every piece of data — event types, hours, bookings — belongs to exactly one tenant, and tenants can never see each other's data.
Event type
A kind of appointment you offer, e.g. “30 Minute Intro Call” or “Haircut”. It defines duration, where it happens, what you ask the customer, and any limits. You can have several.
Availability
Your recurring weekly working hours (e.g. Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00), plus one-off date overrides for exceptions like holidays.
Slot
A specific bookable start time, generated automatically from your availability minus your existing bookings, your minimum-notice rule, and optionally your connected calendar's busy time. Slots are never stored — they're computed fresh on every page load.
Booking, and the invitee
A confirmed appointment. The person who booked is the invitee; they never create an account. They manage their booking through an unguessable private link in their confirmation email.

See the full glossary for the rest of the vocabulary.

Quick tour

End to end, here's what happens:

  1. The owner signs up at /signup — email and password, or “Continue with Google/Microsoft”. A starter event type and default Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00 hours are created automatically, so the account is usable immediately.
  2. They set their real hours at /dashboard/availability, and add date overrides for any days they're closed.
  3. They configure an event type at /dashboard/event-types — duration, location, and on paid plans intake questions, booking caps, approval, and more.
  4. They share one link — /{their-handle}/{event-handle} — by email, on social, or embedded into their own website.
  5. A customer books — they see open slots converted into their own timezone, pick one, fill in name and email, and confirm.
  6. Both sides get an email with a standard .ics calendar attachment, so the appointment lands in Outlook, Apple Calendar or Google Calendar automatically.
  7. Reminders go out automatically 24 hours and 1 hour before the appointment.
  8. Either side can change it — the customer via their private link with no login, the owner from /dashboard/bookings. Cancelling instantly frees the slot for someone else.

Business Owner Guide

Everything you can do with your Bookify account. No technical knowledge assumed.

Your first 15 minutes

Do these five things and you're taking bookings.

  1. Check your timezone first Go to Settings and confirm the Timezone field matches where you actually work. Everything else — your hours, your slots, your reminders — is calculated from this. Getting it wrong means customers book at the wrong times, so do it before anything else.
  2. Set your weekly hours Go to Availability. Tick the days you work and set a start and end time for each. Untick a day to close it entirely. Save.
  3. Create your first event type Go to Event Types. Give it a name your customers will understand — “30 Minute Consultation”, not “Meeting Type A” — pick a duration, and create it. Then click Edit to set where it happens and any extra options.
  4. Test it yourself From the Overview page click Open booking page, and book a slot as though you were a customer. Check the confirmation email arrives and looks right. Then cancel it from Bookings.
  5. Share your link Copy the booking link from Overview and put it in your email signature, your social bios, and your website. If you'd rather have the booking form inside your own site, use Embed instead.
One link or many? Sharing /{your-handle} shows customers all your event types and lets them choose. Sharing /{your-handle}/{event-handle} takes them straight into one specific appointment type — use that when you already know what you're booking them for.

Availability

Availability answers one question: when are you open? Bookify then works out the individual slots.

Weekly hours

At Availability, each day of the week has a checkbox and a start/end time. Tick the day to open it, untick to close it. One time window per day is supported.

All times are in your business timezone, shown at the top of the page with a link to change it. Customers never see your timezone — they see the same slots converted into theirs.

Date overrides

Overrides handle exceptions without you having to edit and then restore your weekly grid. Two kinds:

TypeEffectUse it for
Closed all dayNo slots at all on that date, regardless of your weekly hoursPublic holidays, vacation days, sick days
Custom hoursReplaces that date's weekly hours with the times you specify“Open 10:00–14:00 this one Saturday”, a half-day before a holiday

Only upcoming overrides are listed — past ones drop off automatically.

How a slot actually gets offered

For a given day, a slot appears to a customer only if it survives every one of these checks:

  1. It falls inside your weekly hours for that weekday, or the custom hours from a date override.
  2. The date isn't marked Closed all day.
  3. It's far enough in the future to satisfy the event type's minimum notice.
  4. It doesn't overlap an existing booking that's holding the time.
  5. The event type's daily, weekly and monthly caps haven't already been reached.
  6. For a team event type, the right team member — or all of them, for collective — is free.
  7. You're not marked busy at that time on your connected Google Calendar BUSINESS, if you enabled busy-sync.
Why a slot can vanish between page load and booking. Slots are computed live, and two people can be looking at the same one. Whoever confirms first gets it; the other sees “Sorry, that time was just booked. Pick another.” This is deliberate — it's what makes double-booking impossible.

Event types

An event type is one kind of appointment. Create them at Event Types, then click Edit to configure. Here is every setting, what it does, and what it needs.

Each event type also has Copy link, Clone, Deactivate/Activate, and Delete on its card. Cloning duplicates every setting onto a new event type (so you can build variations of a popular offering without starting over); it's still subject to your plan's event-type limit and, if a setting is only available on a higher plan than you're currently on, the clone drops it rather than silently copying something you're not entitled to.

Intake questions PRO

Add your own questions to the booking form — “What would you like to discuss?”, “Which service?”, “Any accessibility needs?”. Each question can be marked required, in which case the customer can't submit without answering.

Answers appear on the booking in your dashboard and in the CSV export. Required answers are enforced on the server, so a required question can't be skipped by anyone tampering with the page.

After-booking behaviour PRO

SettingWhat it does

Reply-to emailWhen a customer hits Reply on their confirmation, it goes here — e.g. a shared support inbox instead of your personal address.

Manual approval PRO

Turn this on and new bookings arrive as Pending instead of confirmed. You get an email, and an “Awaiting approval” section appears at the top of your Bookings page with Approve and Decline buttons.

Important: a pending booking still holds the time slot, so two people can't both be waiting for approval on the same slot. Approving sends the normal confirmation and calendar invite; declining notifies the customer and frees the slot.

Approval and paid bookings are mutually exclusive — one gate at a time, by design.

Price BUSINESS

Set an amount to charge at booking. Only available for straightforward one-to-one appointments — not group, not recurring, not team — and only once your payments application is approved and your provider onboarding is finished. See Accepting payments.

Group sessions BUSINESS

Normal event types generate slots automatically from your weekly hours, and one booking takes the slot. A group event type works differently: you create each session by hand, and many customers book into the same one.

Use it for classes, workshops, webinars, group tours — anything with a fixed start time and a number of seats.

  1. Turn the event type into a group event Edit the event type and set a capacity, the default number of seats. Saving with a capacity converts it to a group event type.
  2. Create your sessions A Sessions section appears on the edit page. Add each occurrence with its date, time and seat count. Each session snapshots its own duration and capacity, so changing the event type later never retroactively shortens or shrinks a session you've already published.
  3. Customers pick a session Instead of a time-slot grid, the booking page lists your upcoming sessions with remaining seats. Full sessions show as full.
Overbooking is impossible. Seats are claimed with a single atomic database operation that refuses to go past capacity. Even with dozens of people confirming at the exact same instant, you can never sell seat 21 of a 20-seat class. Cancelling a group booking returns the seat immediately.

Recurring bookings BUSINESS

Enable Allow recurring bookings on a one-to-one event type and customers can book a weekly series — same weekday, same time — up to 8 occurrences, in a single step. Ideal for weekly coaching, therapy, lessons or standing check-ins.

  • All or nothing. If any week in the requested series isn't available, the whole series is refused and nothing is booked — you never end up with a half-booked series.
  • Daylight-saving safe. Occurrences are generated so a 10:00 appointment stays at 10:00 local time even across a clock change, rather than drifting by an hour.
  • Each week is its own booking. They're linked as a series and labelled “Week 2/4” in your dashboard, but each can be cancelled or rescheduled individually. The customer's manage page also offers “cancel all remaining”.
  • Mutually exclusive with group. An event type can be group or recurring, not both.

Managing bookings

Bookings shows everything, split into Awaiting approval (if you use approval), Upcoming, and Past. Every entry shows the time in your timezone, the customer's name and email, their notes, their intake answers, any extra guests, and the video link if there is one.

What you can do

  • Approve or decline pending bookings.
  • Reschedule or cancel any booking — you're never blocked by the customer-facing notice window.
  • Add a booking manually PRO for someone who phoned or walked in, choosing whether to email them. Manual bookings deliberately ignore your own minimum-notice and cap rules — you already agreed to the appointment, so the app doesn't argue — but real conflicts (an existing booking, calendar busy time, a closed date) are still enforced.
  • Export to CSV PRO — every booking with times, status, customer, team member, video link, series info, notes, reschedule count and creation date.
Cancelling always frees the slot. Because slot generation only counts bookings that hold time, a cancellation immediately opens the slot back up for someone else. There's no separate “release” step.

What your customer sees

Worth understanding, because it's most of your customer's impression of your business.

  1. Your booking page With your branding FREE — colour, font, logo, welcome message. They pick an event type, or land straight on one, then a date, then a time.
  2. Times in their timezone Bookify detects their timezone and converts every slot into it, with a selector if they want to change it. Nobody has to do mental arithmetic.
  3. A short form Name and email, plus your intake questions, optional notes, and extra guests if you allow them.
  4. Confirmation Either Bookify's confirmation screen or your own redirect page, then an email with an .ics calendar attachment.
  5. Self-service afterwards Their email contains a private link to reschedule or cancel — no account, no password. Rescheduling re-checks your live availability, so they can only move to a time you're genuinely free.
Calendar invites update themselves. When a booking is rescheduled or cancelled, the follow-up email carries an updated calendar attachment that modifies or removes the original entry in the customer's calendar app, instead of leaving a stale duplicate behind.

Branding & embed

Branding FREE

At Branding you can set:

  • Accent colour — used for buttons and highlights on your booking page.
  • Font — chosen from a curated list of web fonts.
  • Logo — shown at the top of your booking page.
  • Welcome message — a short line of copy to greet visitors.

You can save these on any plan; they only display while your plan includes custom branding. On a plan without it, your booking page falls back to Bookify defaults and your saved values wait untouched.

The embed widget FREE

At Embed you get copy-paste snippets with a live preview. Two modes:

  • Inline — the booking form sits in your page and auto-resizes to fit its content, so there's no awkward internal scrollbar.
  • Popup — a button on your site opens the booking form in an overlay.

The loader script is dependency-free — it doesn't need jQuery, React or anything else — and works on WordPress, Squarespace, Webflow, plain HTML, and most site builders that let you paste an embed code. You can override the accent colour and font per-embed with data attributes, so the widget can match the site it's living in rather than your Bookify settings.

Your subscription

At Billing you can compare plans, upgrade, and open the payment portal to change your card, download invoices, or cancel.

  • Upgrading takes you to a secure hosted checkout. Your plan updates automatically once payment succeeds.
  • Promo codes can be entered at upgrade time. Trial codes activate instantly; discount codes apply at checkout.
  • Cancelling keeps your plan active until the end of the period you've paid for. The page shows “ends on” rather than “renews on” once you've cancelled, and you can resume before that date.
Your plan is always read from the server. Feature access is decided by the stored record of your subscription, never by anything the browser claims — so plan state can't be spoofed.

Accepting payments BUSINESS

This is charging your customers when they book — separate from your own Bookify subscription. Because it involves handling other people's money, it's a reviewed, multi-step process.

  1. Apply In Settings → Accept payments, submit your country, a description of your business, and your expected price range.
  2. Wait for review A platform admin approves or rejects, with a reason. Re-applying after a rejection creates a fresh application, keeping the history intact.
  3. Pick a provider and onboard Which providers you can use depends on your country — Razorpay for India, Stripe elsewhere, by default. You complete the provider's own hosted onboarding; Bookify never sees your bank details.
  4. Price an event type Once onboarding reports you're ready to accept charges, a price field appears on eligible event types.
  5. Customers pay at booking The slot is held while they're at checkout. Successful payment confirms the booking; if they abandon it, the hold expires after 30 minutes and the slot reopens.

When you actually get the money

Funds are held briefly rather than paid out instantly, so a same-day problem can still be refunded cleanly.

Customer pays→ HELD→ 24h after the appointment ends → RELEASED to you

Refunds work from either side of that line: while funds are still held, the refund comes straight out of the held amount; after release, it's pulled back. A release that fails — say a provider account problem — is retried automatically up to 5 times and then surfaced to the platform admin with the provider's error message, rather than silently disappearing.

You can't switch payment providers while money is in flight. If any booking is mid-checkout or still holding funds, switching is blocked until those settle. Historical bookings always keep the provider they were paid through, so refunds go back the way they came.

Calendar feed (subscribe, no connection needed)

Settings → Calendar feed gives you a private, read-only web-calendar URL — one for all your bookings, and one per event type. Paste it into Google Calendar, Apple Calendar or Outlook as a subscription (not an import) and your bookings show up there automatically, refreshing on their own. Unlike Meet/Zoom above, this needs no OAuth connection at all — the link itself is the only credential.

Treat the feed link like a password. The link itself is the only credential — anyone who has it can see everything on it: booking times, customer name and email, notes, and the meeting link. If it ever leaks, click Generate new link — the old URL stops working immediately and every calendar subscribed to it will simply stop updating.

Account security

  • Two-factor authentication — at Settings → Security, scan a QR code with any authenticator app. You must enter a valid code to finish enabling it, so you can't accidentally lock yourself out. You also get one-time backup codes for when your phone isn't available; each works once.
  • Sign in with Google or Microsoft — usable alongside a password, or on its own. If you originally signed up with a password, signing in with Google on the same verified email links to your existing account rather than creating a duplicate.
  • Email verification — new password signups must confirm their email before using the dashboard. Accounts created through Google or Microsoft are already verified by the provider.
  • Password changes end other sessions — changing your password signs out every other device immediately and emails you a notification, so a stolen session can be cut off.
  • Automatic lockout — after 5 failed password attempts the account locks for 15 minutes and you're emailed about it. The error tells you how many minutes are left.

Deleting your account

Deletion is deliberately slow, with two separate chances to undo it.

  1. You request deletion Nothing is deleted. Your account stays fully active and your booking page keeps working. A banner appears in your dashboard with a one-click “keep my account” undo — no token or email needed, since you're already signed in. This grace period lasts 48 hours.
  2. The grace period passes Now the real work happens: your subscription is cancelled, upcoming bookings are cancelled with your customers notified, video meetings are removed, and your booking page goes offline. You're emailed a recovery link.
  3. The recovery window For 30 days, that emailed link restores your account and brings your booking page back. Cancelled bookings and your cancelled subscription do not come back — only the account itself.
  4. Permanent deletion After 30 days everything is purged for good. Nothing can be recovered past this point.
The distinction that matters. During the first 48 hours nothing is lost and undo is instant. After that, your bookings and subscription are genuinely gone even if you recover the account. If you're only unsure, cancel the request inside the 48 hours.

Platform Admin Guide

For whoever runs Bookify itself — managing every business on the platform, at /admin.

Roles & permissions

Admin access is granted per account and is never self-serve — it has to be set directly in the database by someone with database access. There is deliberately no “request admin” button anywhere in the product.

To anyone without an admin role, /admin returns a plain “page not found”. It doesn't say “access denied”, because that would confirm the console exists.

Every change is logged and every check is server-side. Every mutating action also writes an entry to the audit log that can't be edited or deleted.

Your first admin session

Opening /admin for the first time gives you a dense analytics dashboard, which is useful later but not a great starting point. Do this instead:

  1. Set up email delivery Communications → Email settings. Until this is configured, Bookify writes emails to the server log instead of sending them — which means no verification emails, no booking confirmations, and no reminders reach anyone. This is the single most common “why is nothing working” cause.
  2. Set up billing credentials Platform setup → Billing settings. Enter your payment provider keys. Note there are separate slots for test and live credentials and an explicit switch between them — they are never mixed.
  3. Check your plans Platform setup → Plans. Confirm each plan's price and, importantly, that its feature gates match its pricing-page bullets. See Editing plans — this is easy to get subtly wrong.
  4. Review sign-in providers Platform setup → Sign-in providers if you want Google/Microsoft login. Each provider also needs a matching redirect URL configured on their side.
  5. Confirm the reminder scheduler is running Reminders are pull-based and won't fire on their own. See Scheduled jobs.
  6. Now look at the Overview With the platform actually working, the metrics start meaning something.

Operations

Overview

The landing page is a business dashboard over a 7, 30 or 90 day window:

  • Headline numbers — monthly recurring revenue and annualised run rate, paying users and conversion rate, average revenue per user, churn, activation, active accounts, bookings and signups.
  • Trend charts — revenue, new signups and bookings created over the window.
  • Activation funnel — how many accounts signed up → set availability → created an event type → received a booking → upgraded to paid. The biggest drop-off tells you where onboarding is failing.
  • Plan mix and top accounts by bookings and by revenue.
Revenue history is approximate. Bookify doesn't store a history of upgrades and downgrades, so the revenue-over-time chart is reconstructed from current plans and signup dates. Treat the trend as directional, not as accounting.

Users

The tenant list, with search across business name, email and handle, plus filters for plan, “has bookings”, and “show deleted”. Columns are sortable, results are paginated, and the whole filtered set can be exported to CSV.

Clicking an account opens its detail page:

  • Plan, subscription status, renewal or end date, and cancellation date if they've cancelled
  • Their public booking link, total and upcoming booking counts
  • A preview of their branding and their weekly availability grid
  • Their event types and paginated booking history
  • A link through to their customer record at the payment provider
  • Internal notes — visible only to admins, never shown to the tenant. Use these for support context.
  • The admin action panel — plan change, password reset, suspend, role, and the danger zone

Bookings

A cross-tenant view of every booking on the platform. Filter by search, status, business and date range; sort columns; export to CSV. A stats strip shows cancellation rate and reschedule rate.

You can also suspend a booking's business directly from a row, which is the fast path when you're responding to an abuse report.

This is the one place that reads across tenants on purpose. Everywhere else in the product, queries are locked to a single tenant. The admin console is the deliberate exception.

Approving a payment application is a real trust decision. You're letting that business take money from the public through your platform. The application includes their country, business description and expected price range specifically so you have something to judge. Rejections carry a reason, which the tenant sees.

Communications

Announcements

A banner shown to every tenant inside their dashboard. Three tones — info, warning, critical — and an optional auto-expiry date so you don't have to remember to switch it off. Only active announcements render.

Broadcast

One-off messaging out to tenants, for things that shouldn't wait for them to log in.

Email settings

Choose how outgoing mail is actually delivered. Options include Gmail SMTP with an app password, Amazon SES SMTP credentials, or falling back to server environment variables. You can also set global email branding — the name, logo, accent colour, footer text and support link used in the shared shell that wraps every email.

If no email provider is configured, nothing is sent. Emails are written to the server log instead. The app deliberately keeps working without email — a booking still succeeds even if its confirmation can't be sent — which is convenient in development and dangerous in production, because it fails silently. Configure this first.

Email templates

All 19 transactional emails (listed in Email notifications) are editable here, with subject, HTML body and plain-text body, plus a live preview. Each template supports a fixed set of {{variable}} placeholders appropriate to that email — a booking confirmation can use the customer's name and the appointment time, for example.

You can't break email delivery by editing a template. Every send site has a hardcoded fallback. If a template row is missing, disabled, or malformed, the built-in default is used instead, so the email still goes out.

Editing plans

Each plan has two things that look similar and behave completely differently. Getting these out of sync is the most common way to mislead your own customers.

FieldWhat it actually does

checkbox grid		The real thing. Each checkbox has a matching server-side check. Ticking or unticking one changes what accounts on that plan can actually do, immediately, with no redeploy.
These two are not linked. Update both. If you tick a new feature gate but don't add a matching bullet, customers won't know they have it. If you untick a gate but leave the bullet, your pricing page is advertising something the plan no longer does — which is the worse direction. The editor warns about this inline, but nothing enforces it.

Other notes on plans:

  • Plan ID is permanent. It's stored on every account using that plan, so it can't be renamed after creation. The display name can change freely.
  • The Free plan is a system plan. It always exists, always costs zero, and can't be deleted or deactivated, because it's the safe fallback for any account whose plan can't be resolved.
  • You can create entirely new tiers with any subset of the fifteen capabilities — you're not limited to Free/Pro/Business.
  • A plan can't be deleted while accounts are on it. Move them off first; the app blocks the deletion and tells you how many are affected.
  • Hiding a plan removes it from the pricing page without affecting accounts already on it — the right way to retire a tier.

An append-only record of every mutating admin action: who did it, to which account, what changed, and when. Entries cannot be edited or deleted — including by super admins.

Architecture

Server-first, and no API layer

Data is read directly in async server components using Prisma. Mutations go through server actions. There is intentionally no REST or tRPC layer.

The /api directory exists only for things that genuinely cannot be a page or an action: OAuth callbacks, payment webhooks, cron endpoints, file upload and binary/CSV responses.

Client components are the exception rather than the rule — used only where real interactivity is required (auth forms, the booking widget, the copy-link button, the mobile drawer).

Multi-tenancy

Tenancy is row-level by userId. Every event type, availability window and booking belongs to one tenant. The public site keys off the tenant's URL handle, and every dashboard action re-checks ownership in its where clause rather than trusting an ID from the form.

The admin console is the single deliberate exception that reads across tenants.

Auth

Sessions are never delegated to a third party, even when the user authenticates with Google or Microsoft. A signed JWT is issued and stored in an httpOnly cookie regardless of how they signed in.

Social login is a hand-rolled Authorization Code flow rather than a library. It verifies the returned identity token's signature against the provider's published keys — not merely trusting a userinfo response — and links to an existing account only by a verified email address. An unverified provider email is rejected outright rather than auto-linked, because auto-linking on an unverified address is an account-takeover vector.

Sessions carry a version number. Bumping the stored version — on password change, suspension, or admin force-logout — invalidates every existing token instantly.

Timezone model

Two different representations, deliberately:

  • Availability is timezone-naive — stored as a weekday plus minutes-from-midnight. “Monday, 540 to 1020” means 09:00–17:00 in whatever the tenant's timezone currently is. If they move timezone, their working hours move with them, which is what people expect.
  • Bookings are absolute UTC instants. A booked appointment is a fixed moment in time and must not shift if anyone's timezone setting changes.

Slot generation is the bridge: it takes the tenant's wall-clock availability, interprets it in their IANA timezone for a specific date, and produces UTC instants. Rendering goes the other way, formatting the stored UTC instant into whichever timezone the viewer is in.

Do not "simplify" this into one representation. Storing availability in UTC breaks daylight-saving (a 9am start would drift to 8am or 10am for half the year). Storing bookings as wall-clock breaks the moment a timezone setting changes. Both halves are load-bearing.

Feature entitlements

A code-defined registry lists every gateable capability. Each plan holds an admin-editable array of the keys it grants. One helper resolves “does this plan include this capability?” and every gate in the app calls it — never a direct plan-name comparison like plan === "BUSINESS", which is what made this hard to change before.

The registry lives in code rather than the database on purpose: a key is only meaningful if a matching server-side check exists somewhere. An admin can turn an existing gate on or off per plan, but can't invent a new gate from the UI that nothing enforces.

State machines

Booking status

Three of the four statuses hold the time slot. Only CANCELLED releases it — which is why cancelling frees a slot with no extra bookkeeping.

PENDING— owner approves → CONFIRMED
PENDING— owner declines → CANCELLED
PENDING_PAYMENT— payment webhook → CONFIRMED
PENDING_PAYMENT— 30 min with no payment → CANCELLED
CONFIRMED— either side cancels → CANCELLED

Approval-required and paid are mutually exclusive, so a booking is never both PENDING and PENDING_PAYMENT.

Payout lifecycle

Independent of the booking's own status — money state and slot state are tracked separately on purpose.

HELD— 24h after appointment ends → RELEASED
HELD— refund while held → REFUNDED
RELEASED— refund after release → REVERSED
HELD— transfer fails → RELEASE_FAILED— retried, max 5 attempts → RELEASED

Fee and amount are snapshotted at release time, so later changes to the platform's fee percentage never rewrite historical payouts. After 5 failed attempts a booking stops being retried and is surfaced in the admin payments view with the provider's error message.

Scheduling engine

The most delicate code in the project. Changes here deserve real care.

How slot generation works

  1. Look up the tenant's availability windows for the requested date's weekday.
  2. Apply any date override — bail out entirely if the date is blocked, or substitute custom hours.
  3. Walk the window in steps, producing candidate wall-clock start times.
  4. Convert each wall-clock time to a UTC instant in the tenant's timezone for that specific date, so daylight-saving is handled correctly.
  5. Drop anything already in the past, or inside the event type's minimum-notice horizon.
  6. Drop everything for the day if it's beyond the event type's booking window (maxAdvanceDays).
  7. Drop anything overlapping an existing booking whose status holds the slot — widened by the event type's padding, if any, so a booking blocks [start − padding, end + padding] rather than just its own window.
  8. Drop everything for the day if a daily, weekly or monthly cap is already met.
  9. For team event types, intersect against member availability — any member for round-robin, all members for collective.
  10. Subtract connected-calendar busy windows, if the tenant has busy-sync enabled and their plan still grants it.

Double-booking prevention

Two independent layers, because the first one alone is a race condition:

  1. At read time — booked times aren't offered as slots.
  2. At write time — an overlap re-check runs inside the same transaction as the insert. If someone else took the slot in the seconds between page load and submit, the booking is refused with “that time was just booked”.

Group sessions use a different mechanism suited to their shape: seats are claimed with a single conditional update that only succeeds while seats remain, making overbooking impossible even under heavy concurrency.

Plan gates are enforced at read time, not by clearing settings. Calendar busy-sync checks the tenant's current plan at the moment busy data is fetched. It does not switch off the tenant's syncBusyTimes toggle on downgrade — so if they upgrade again, their preference is still there. The same principle applies everywhere: gates block new usage; they never destroy stored settings.

Adding a feature gate

The recipe, in order. Skipping step 2 is the classic mistake — a hidden control is not a gate.

  1. Add the key to the registry Add an entry to the feature registry in src/lib/features.ts with a key, a human label and a one-line description. The admin checkbox grid and plan-seed defaults both read from this array, so nothing else needs updating for it to appear in the admin UI.
  2. Enforce it server-side In the server action (or route handler, or data-fetching function) call the plan helper and refuse or drop the gated input when it returns false. This is the actual gate. Treat form input as hostile — assume the field was submitted even though the UI didn't render it.
  3. Preserve existing data on downgrade When the gate is closed, fall back to the row's existing value rather than a hardcoded default. Otherwise a downgraded tenant who edits an unrelated field silently wipes settings they configured on a higher plan.
  4. Hide the control in the UI Optional but expected — render an upgrade note in place of the gated control. This is a courtesy for the user, never a security boundary.
  5. Grant it on the right plans Tick the new checkbox on whichever plans should include it, at /admin/settings/plans, and add a matching pricing-page bullet.
  6. Verify npx tsc --noEmit and npm run build. Then check the downgrade path specifically — set an account to a plan without the gate, edit something unrelated, and confirm the gated data survived.

Scheduled jobs

Two endpoints must be called on a schedule. Nothing in the app triggers them by itself — if no scheduler is configured, reminders never send and deletions never progress.

EndpointDoesSuggested frequency
/api/cron/remindersSends due 24-hour and 1-hour reminder emails. The two windows don't overlap, so a booking never gets both at once.Every few minutes

Both accept the shared secret as a bearer token or query parameter, and both are idempotent — overlapping or repeated calls are safe, because completed work is stamped and skipped. A successful reminder call returns the counts it sent.

Hosting-plan gotcha. The committed schedule runs reminders every 10 minutes, which requires a paid hosting tier. Free tiers often permit only once-daily scheduled jobs — in which case use any external ping service or a system crontab hitting the same URL instead. A once-daily reminder job would make 1-hour reminders useless.

Conventions & gotchas

This is not the Next.js you may remember The project's AGENTS.md flags that this Next.js version has breaking changes from what older documentation and training data assume. Check the bundled docs under node_modules/next/dist/docs/ before using an unfamiliar API.
  • Mutations are server actions. New ones belong in an actions.ts beside the page that uses them, marked "use server". Don't add REST endpoints for things an action can do.
  • Always re-check ownership. Every tenant mutation filters by the current user's ID. Never trust a record ID from a form on its own.
  • UI hiding is never a security boundary. Any control hidden by a plan gate or a role check must also be enforced in the code that receives the submission.
  • Downgrades preserve data. When adding a gated field, the “not entitled” branch falls back to the existing stored value, not a default. Wiping a user's configuration because they changed plans is a bug.
  • Store UTC, interpret in the tenant's timezone. Don't collapse the two-representation model described in Architecture.
  • Test and live payment credentials never mix. There's no fallback between modes — a missing key in the active mode is an error, not a reason to reach for the other set.
  • Tokens, not logins, for one-off invitee flows. Booking management, password reset and account recovery all use a random single-purpose token in an emailed link. Follow that pattern rather than inventing guest sessions.
  • Email must never block the primary action. A booking succeeds even if its confirmation email throws. Wrap sends so a mail failure is logged, not fatal.
  • Log with the structured logger. Use logger rather than console.* so production logs stay queryable.

Deployment

  1. Provision Postgres Get both a pooled and an unpooled (direct) connection string.
  2. Set environment variables Database URLs, a fresh production AUTH_SECRET, the real NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL, and a fresh CRON_SECRET. Payment and mail credentials are optional here since they're configured in the admin console.
  3. Deploy Push and import the repo into your host. The build runs prisma generate automatically and doesn't need a live database, though the running app does.
  4. Apply migrations Run npm run db:deploy once against production to create the schema.
  5. Grant yourself admin Set your account's admin role directly in the production database.
  6. Configure the platform In /admin: email delivery first, then payment credentials, then check plans. No redeploy needed for any of it.
  7. Set up the schedulers Point a scheduler at both cron endpoints — see Scheduled jobs — and verify reminders actually arrive.
Changing your domain later? Update NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL and redeploy, then separately update the redirect URLs registered with Google, Microsoft and Zoom. Those live on each provider's side and won't follow your env var — a mismatch breaks social login and calendar connections with a confusing error.

FAQ & troubleshooting

Nothing is sending emails

By far the most common problem. Check Admin → Communications → Email settings. With no provider configured, Bookify writes every email to the server log instead of sending it — deliberately, so the app runs with zero config in development. In production that means silent failure: no verification emails, no booking confirmations, no reminders. Configure a provider, then send a test.

Reminder emails never arrive, but other emails work

Reminders are pull-based — a scheduler has to call /api/cron/reminders every few minutes. Check three things: that a scheduler is actually configured, that it's using the correct shared secret, and that your hosting tier permits the frequency you set. A free tier limited to daily jobs cannot deliver a 1-hour reminder. Also confirm the email_reminders global flag is on.

“Sign in with Google/Microsoft” broke after I changed domain

The redirect URL is registered on the provider's side and does not follow your NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL. Update it in the Google Cloud Console / Azure portal to match the new domain exactly, including scheme and any trailing path.

Booking links or embed snippets show an old domain

Those are built from NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL at request time. Update the environment variable on your host and redeploy — it's not stored per-tenant.

A tenant says a feature is missing even though they're on the right plan

Check both layers. First the global feature flag — one switched off during an incident and never restored will hide a feature from everyone regardless of plan. Then the plan's feature gate checkbox at /admin/settings/plans. A plan's marketing bullets are not proof it grants a capability; only the checkbox is.

The pricing page describes a plan incorrectly

The bullets and the actual feature gates are separate fields that nothing keeps in sync. Someone changed one without the other. Fix the bullets at /admin/settings/plans to match the ticked checkboxes — and see Editing plans.

Payments behave differently than expected

Check which mode is active in Admin → Platform setup → Billing settings. Test and live credentials are stored separately and never mixed, so it's easy to be looking at the wrong provider dashboard. Confirm the mode there matches the dashboard you're checking.

A tenant can't switch payment provider

Expected if they have money in flight — a booking mid-checkout, or funds still held awaiting release. Switching unblocks once those settle. Check Admin → Revenue → Payments to see what's outstanding.

A payout didn't reach a tenant

Look for a failed release in Admin → Revenue → Payments. Releases are retried up to 5 times, then stop and surface the provider's error message — usually an incomplete provider account on the tenant's side. The message tells you which.

Slots aren't appearing on a booking page

Work down the list in How a slot actually gets offered. The usual causes, roughly in order of likelihood: no weekly hours saved for that weekday; a date override marking the day closed; a minimum-notice window swallowing the near-term slots; a daily/weekly/monthly cap already reached; connected-calendar busy time covering the day; or, for a team event type, no assigned members with matching availability.

Times look wrong to the tenant or their customers

Check the tenant's timezone in Settings first — everything derives from it. Remember customers deliberately see times converted into their own timezone, so the same slot legitimately reads differently to different people. That's not a bug.

A tenant lost settings after a plan change

They shouldn't — downgrades are designed to preserve data and only block creating new gated things. If configuration genuinely disappeared, that's a bug worth investigating rather than expected behaviour; see the downgrade rule in Adding a feature gate.

A tenant deleted their account by mistake

Timing decides what's possible. Inside the first 48 hours, they can undo it themselves from the banner in their dashboard and lose nothing. After that, the recovery link emailed to them restores the account for 30 days — but their cancelled bookings and subscription do not come back. After 30 days, nothing can be recovered.

A booking page shows “not found”

Either the handle is wrong, or the account is suspended or deleted — suspended accounts have their public pages taken offline deliberately. Check the account's status in Admin → Users.

Signups are turned off and prospects are bouncing

That's the signupsEnabled switch in Admin → Platform setup → Platform config. While it's off, the signup page explains that registration is paused and shows your support email if one is configured — so set a support email if you're going to leave signups closed for a while.

Glossary

Availability
A tenant's recurring weekly working hours. Stored as weekday plus minutes-from-midnight, interpreted in their timezone.
Booking
One appointment. Stored as an absolute UTC instant, rendered in whichever timezone the viewer is in.
Date override
A one-off exception to the weekly grid for a single date — either closed all day, or replacement hours.
Escrow / hold
The period after a customer pays but before the money is released to the tenant — 24 hours after the appointment ends, so a same-day problem can still be refunded cleanly.
Event type
A kind of bookable appointment, with its own duration, location, questions and limits.
Feature flag
A platform-wide on/off switch for a capability, independent of plans. Used as an emergency kill-switch or for staged rollout.
Feature gate
A per-plan entitlement checkbox with a matching server-side check. This is what actually decides whether a tenant can use something.
Group event type
An event type with a capacity, where the tenant creates each session manually and many customers book into the same one.
Impersonation
A super admin signing in as a tenant to see exactly what they see. Always shows a banner and is recorded in the audit log.
Invitee
The customer who books. Never has an account; manages their booking through a private emailed link.
Manage token
The unguessable random string in a booking's private link. The same pattern is used for password resets and account recovery.
Minimum notice
How far in advance a customer must book. Hides slots that are too soon.
Round-robin / collective
Team assignment modes. Round-robin rotates bookings between members; collective requires everyone to be free and attend.
Slot
A bookable start time. Computed live on every page load, never stored.
Soft delete vs hard delete
Soft delete hides an account and blocks login but erases nothing, and is reversible indefinitely. Hard delete is permanent and cascades through all the account's data.
Suspension
Blocking an account's login and taking its public pages offline, without touching its data. Instantly reversible.
Tenant
One business account on the platform. The unit of data isolation.
Session (group)
One dated occurrence of a group event type, with its own capacity and seat count. Not to be confused with a login session.
